# Topic 3.2 · Relative & Absolute Time
**Specification Section:** Fundamentals  
**Key Topics:** Principles of stratigraphy, way-up criteria, zone fossils, radiometric decay, geological timescale  

---

## Principles of Relative Dating

- **Law of Superposition:** In undeformed sedimentary/volcanic sequence, oldest bed at bottom, youngest at top.
- **Principle of Original Horizontality:** Sediments are deposited under gravity in flat, horizontal layers; tilting/folding occurred post-deposition.
- **Cross-cutting relationships:** Any feature (fault, igneous intrusion, mineral vein, unconformity) that cuts across another rock unit is **younger** than the rock it cuts.
- **Principle of Inclusions:** Fragments (xenoliths in igneous rocks; pebbles in conglomerates) are always **older** than the rock containing them.

## Way-Up Evidence (Top/Bottom Criteria)

- **Sedimentary way-up:** Cross-bedding truncation (eroded at top), Graded bedding (coarse at bottom, fine at top), Ripple crests (point up), Mudcracks (V points down), Flute casts/load casts (bulbous downward projections on bed base).
- **Volcanic way-up:** Pillow lavas (convex rounded top, cusped/keeled base fitting into underlying pillow), baked margin on bottom of flow only, vesicular zone at flow top.
- **Paleontological way-up:** Plant roots branching downwards; corals growing upright.

## Biostratigraphy & Ideal Zone Fossils

- **Characteristics of an ideal Index / Zone Fossil:**
- 1. **Rapid evolution:** Short stratigraphic range (precise dating).
- 2. **Geographically widespread:** Found across basins and continents.
- 3. **Abundant:** Easily found in rock exposures and drill cores.
- 4. **Easily identifiable:** Distinct morphological features.
- 5. **Facies-independent:** Pelagic / planktonic (floated in water column, found in many sediment types).
- **Best index fossils:** Graptolites (Ordovician–Silurian), Ammonites (Devonian–Cretaceous), Microfossils/Conodonts/Foraminifera (Phanerozoic).

## Radiometric Absolute Dating

- **Radioactive decay:** Unstable parent isotope decays to stable radiogenic daughter isotope at a constant exponential rate unaffected by heat, pressure, or chemistry.
- **Half-life (t1/2):** Time taken for half the radioactive parent atoms to decay.
- • Decay stages: 0 half-lives (100% P) -> 1 (50% P, 50% D) -> 2 (25% P, 75% D) -> 3 (12.5% P, 87.5% D).
- **Closure temperature:** The temperature below which isotopes can no longer diffuse out of the crystal lattice. If rock is reheated during metamorphism above closure temp, radiogenic daughter escapes and radiometric clock is **reset**.
- **Key isotope systems:**
- • *K-Ar / Ar-Ar:* K-40 decays to Ar-40 (t1/2 = 1.25 Ga). Dating igneous/metamorphic biotite, hornblende, sanidine.
- • *U-Pb:* U-238/U-235 in Zircon. Zircon has very high closure temp (>900°C), retains primary crystallisation age.
- • *Carbon-14:* t1/2 = 5,730 yrs. Only dates organic material up to ~50,000 years (Quaternary).

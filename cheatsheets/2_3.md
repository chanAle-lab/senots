## Stress, Strain & Orientation

- **Strike:** The compass trend of a horizontal line on a dipping planar surface (e.g. 045°).
- **Dip:** The maximum angle of inclination measured perpendicular to strike downwards from the horizontal (e.g. 30° SE).
- **Apparent dip:** The inclination measured in any non-perpendicular direction to strike; always less than true dip.
- **Brittle vs Ductile deformation:** Low temp/confining pressure + fast strain rate = brittle (faults/joints); High temp/pressure + slow strain rate = ductile (folding/ductile shear).

## Fold Classification & Geometry

- **Anticline:** Convex upwards fold with **oldest strata in core**.
- **Syncline:** Concave upwards fold with **youngest strata in core**.
- **Symmetrical fold:** Axial plane is vertical; limbs dip in opposite directions at equal angles.
- **Asymmetrical fold:** Axial plane is inclined; limbs dip at different angles.
- **Overturned fold:** One limb is tilted beyond vertical so strata are inverted (younger below older).
- **Recumbent fold:** Axial plane is horizontal or sub-horizontal (dip <10°).
- **Plunging fold:** Fold hinge line is inclined to the horizontal; produces V-shaped or U-shaped outcrop pattern on flat terrain. Anticlines plunge towards the closed apex of the V; synclines plunge towards the open end.

## Fault Mechanics & Identification

- **Normal fault:** Extensional stress (\(\sigma_1\) vertical). **Hanging wall moves down** relative to footwall. Lengthens crust. Omits strata on maps.
- **Reverse fault:** Compressional stress (\(\sigma_1\) horizontal). **Hanging wall moves up** relative to footwall (dip >45°). Shortens crust. Duplicates strata.
- **Thrust fault:** Low-angle reverse fault (dip <45°); places older rocks over younger rocks over large lateral distances.
- **Strike-slip (tear) fault:** Shear stress (\(\sigma_2\) vertical). Lateral displacement (dextral = clockwise/right-lateral; sinistral = anticlockwise/left-lateral).
- **Field & map evidence for faulting:** Truncation and offset of beds, slickensides (grooves showing movement direction), fault breccia / fault gouge, drag folds (limbs flexed towards fault movement), spring lines.

## Unconformities & The Rule of V's

- **Angular unconformity:** Gap in geological record where tilted/folded older rocks are eroded and overlain by flatter younger rocks.
- • Evidence: Truncation of beds below, dip discordance, basal conglomerate containing pebbles derived from underlying unit.
- **Rule of V's (Map Outcrop Pattern across Valleys):**
- • Horizontal beds: Contour parallel; outcrop bands follow contours.
- • Vertical beds: Straight lines across topography regardless of valleys/hills.
- • Dipping beds: V points in the direction of dip down a valley, UNLESS the bed dips downstream at a lower angle than the valley gradient.

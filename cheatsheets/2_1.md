## Weathering & Transport

- **Mechanical weathering:** Freeze-thaw (9% water expansion in joints), Exfoliation / onion-skin (insolation thermal expansion/contraction in arid climates), Salt crystallisation.
- **Chemical weathering:**
- • *Carbonation:* Rainwater dissolves CO2 -> carbonic acid (H2CO3) dissolving limestone.
- • *Hydrolysis:* Acidified water attacks feldspar -> kaolinite (china clay) + silicic acid + soluble K+/Na+ ions.
- • *Oxidation:* Iron minerals (e.g. olivine, augite, pyrite) react with oxygen -> hematite / limonite (rust red).
- **Hjulström Curve:** Relates sediment grain size to water flow velocity required for *erosion (entrainment)*, *transport*, and *deposition*.
- • Clay/silt requires higher velocity to entrain than sand due to electrostatic cohesion.
- • Sand entrains at the lowest velocity (~20 cm/s).
- • Deposition occurs whenever velocity drops below settling velocity.

## Textural Maturity & Clastic Classification

- **Grain size (Wentworth scale):** Gravel / Rudaceous (>2 mm), Sand / Arenaceous (0.0625 mm - 2 mm), Mud / Argillaceous (<0.0625 mm, silt and clay).
- **Textural maturity:** Increases with transport distance and time: Poorly sorted + angular + high matrix -> Well sorted + well rounded + low matrix (orthoquartzite).
- **Clastic rock types:**
- • *Breccia:* Angular clasts (>2 mm), near-source deposit (scree / fault talus / wadi flash flood).
- • *Conglomerate:* Rounded clasts (>2 mm), high-energy aqueous transport (beach / high-energy river channel).
- • *Orthoquartzite:* >95% quartz grains, well rounded, well sorted, silica cement; high-energy shallow marine / beach.
- • *Arkose:* >25% feldspar, pink/red, moderate sorting/rounding; rapid deposition from granitic source in semi-arid alluvial fan.
- • *Greywacke:* Poorly sorted, angular grains in dark fine-grained muddy matrix (>15%); submarine turbidity current deposit.

## Sedimentary Structures & Way-Up Criteria

- **Cross-bedding:** Formed by migrating ripples/dunes. Asymptotic curve at base, sharp truncation at top by erosion. *Way-up:* Tops truncated. *Paleocurrent:* Dips down-current.
- **Graded bedding:** Coarse clasts at base fining upward into fine silt/mud (density current / turbidity current settling). *Way-up:* Coarse at base, fine at top.
- **Asymmetrical ripples:** Formed by unidirectional current (river, wind); steep lee slope down-current.
- **Symmetrical ripples:** Formed by bidirectional oscillatory wave action in shallow water; sharp crests point up.
- **Desiccation mudcracks:** V-shaped cracks in mud due to subaerial drying; V points downwards.

## Carbonate & Chemical Sediments & Diagenesis

- **Oolitic limestone:** Formed of spherical ooids (calcite precipitated concentrically around nucleus in warm, agitated, supersaturated shallow marine water).
- **Bioclastic limestone:** Abundant fossil fragments (crinoids, bivalves, corals) cemented by sparry calcite.
- **Chalk:** Pure white micrite of microscopic coccolith plates (calcareous nannoplankton) in quiet pelagic marine shelf.
- **Evaporites:** Halite (NaCl), Gypsum (CaSO4·2H2O), Anhydrite. Sequential precipitation in restricted marine basins with high evaporation > precipitation.
- **Diagenesis stages:**
- 1. Compaction: Expulsion of pore water, reduction of porosity.
- 2. Cementation: Precipitation of mineral matter (calcite, quartz, iron oxide) from pore fluids, binding grains.
- 3. Recrystallisation / Neomorphism: Unstable aragonite converts to stable calcite.

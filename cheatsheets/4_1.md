## Seismic Waves & Propagation

- **P-waves (Primary / Compressional):** Longitudinal particle motion parallel to wave propagation. Travel through solids and liquids. Fastest wave (~6–13 km/s).
- **S-waves (Secondary / Shear):** Transverse particle motion perpendicular to wave propagation. Shear elastic resistance only. **Cannot travel through liquids or gases** (velocity drops to zero). Slower than P-waves (~3.5–7 km/s).
- **Ray Paths & Velocity:** In a homogeneous solid, velocity increases with depth due to rising rigidity and incompressibility exceeding density increase -> seismic rays refract/bend upwards.

## Earth's Internal Discontinuities & Layers

- **Moho (Mohorovičić Discontinuity):** Crust-mantle boundary. P-wave velocity jumps abruptly from ~6.0 km/s (crust) to ~8.0 km/s (peridotite mantle).
- • Continental crust: 30–70 km thick, granitic/felsic, density ~2.7 g/cm³.
- • Oceanic crust: 5–10 km thick, basaltic/gabbroic, density ~3.0 g/cm³.
- **Low Velocity Zone (LVZ / Asthenosphere):** Depth 100–250 km. P and S velocities dip slightly due to 1–2% partial melting; ductile, allows tectonic plate movement.
- **Gutenberg Discontinuity (Core-Mantle Boundary / CMB, 2900 km):**
- • P-wave velocity drops sharply from ~13.7 km/s to ~8.1 km/s.
- • **S-wave velocity drops to zero** -> proves the **outer core is liquid**.
- **Lehmann Discontinuity (Inner Core Boundary, 5150 km):** P-wave velocity jumps from ~10.3 km/s to ~11.2 km/s -> confirms **solid inner core** (high pressure prevents melting).

## Seismic Shadow Zones

- **P-wave shadow zone (103° to 142°):** Caused by severe refraction (bending inwards) of P-waves entering the slower liquid outer core at the Gutenberg discontinuity.
- **S-wave shadow zone (103° to 180°):** Complete absence of direct S-waves beyond 103° on either side of earthquake focus because S-waves cannot travel through the liquid outer core.

## Isostasy & Gravity

- **Isostasy:** Gravitational equilibrium between Earth's lithosphere floating on the denser asthenosphere.
- **Airy hypothesis:** Uniform crustal density, varying root depth (tall mountains have deep buoyant crustal roots).
- **Post-glacial rebound:** Melting of continental ice sheets causes lithosphere to slowly uplift as asthenosphere flows back in.

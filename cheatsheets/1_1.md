## Mineral Definition & Bonding

- **Mineral definition:** A naturally occurring, inorganic crystalline solid with a definite chemical composition and ordered internal atomic structure.
- **Silicate Tetrahedron [SiO4]4-:** Fundamental building block. 1 silicon ion surrounded by 4 oxygen ions in tetrahedral coordination.
- **Bond types:** Covalent (strong, directional, e.g. diamond/quartz), Ionic (transfer of electrons, moderate strength, e.g. halite), Metallic (electron sea, high conductivity, e.g. pyrite), van der Waals (weak intermolecular, cleavage planes in graphite/mica).

## Silicate Frameworks & Cleavage Rules

- **Isolated tetrahedra:** Olivine (Mg,Fe)2SiO4. No cleavage, fracture only. High density.
- **Single chains:** Augite / Pyroxene. Chains bonded by cations. **Two cleavage planes intersecting at 90°** (87°/93°).
- **Double chains:** Hornblende / Amphibole. **Two cleavage planes intersecting at 60° and 120°** (56°/124°).
- **Sheet silicates:** Micas (Biotite / Muscovite). Tetrahedra share 3 oxygens. **One perfect basal cleavage**.
- **3D Framework:** Quartz (SiO2) - shares all 4 oxygens, no cleavage, conchoidal fracture. Feldspars (Orthoclase / Plagioclase) - 2 cleavages at ~90°.

## Diagnostic Properties (Hand Specimen & Thin Section)

- **Hardness (Mohs scale):** Talc(1) < Gypsum(2) < [Fingernail ~2.5] < Calcite(3) < [Copper ~3.5] < Fluorite(4) < Apatite(5) < [Steel/Glass ~5.5] < Orthoclase(6) < Quartz(7) < Topaz(8) < Corundum(9) < Diamond(10).
- **Calcite vs Quartz:** Calcite scratches with copper/nail (H=3), effervesces with dilute cold HCl (CaCO3 + 2HCl -> CaCl2 + H2O + CO2), 3 directions of rhombohedral cleavage. Quartz scratches glass (H=7), no acid reaction, conchoidal fracture.
- **Thin section optical properties:**
- • *Plane polarised light (PPL):* Relief (refractive index vs resin), Pleochroism (colour change on rotation e.g. Biotite brown-yellow, Hornblende green-brown), Cleavage traces.
- • *Crossed polars (XPL):* Birefringence / Interference colours (order of colours on Michel-Lévy chart), Extinction angle (straight/parallel in quartz/orthoclase/biotite, inclined in augite/hornblende), Twinning (cross-hatched in microcline, albite polysynthetic stripes in plagioclase, Carlsbad simple twin in orthoclase).

## Goldich Weathering Series & Stability

- Minerals that crystallise at the highest temperatures and pressures (least like surface conditions) weather the fastest.
- **Susceptibility to chemical weathering:** Olivine (fastest) > Augite > Hornblende > Biotite > Plagioclase > Orthoclase > Muscovite > Quartz (most resistant).
- Quartz and clays dominate mature sedimentary deposits because quartz is mechanically hard and chemically inert.

## Continental Drift & Paleomagnetism

- **Evidence for drift:** Continental shelf jigsaw fit, matching fossil distributions (Glossopteris flora, Mesosaurus), matching mountain belts across Atlantic, late Paleozoic glacial striations and tillites across southern continents (Gondwana).
- **Remanent Magnetism:** As mafic lava cools below the **Curie point of magnetite (~580°C)**, magnetic dipoles align with Earth's current magnetic field and lock in.
- **Seafloor Spreading (Vine-Matthews-Morley):** Symmetrical marine magnetic anomalies ('stripes' of normal and reversed polarities) parallel to mid-ocean ridges. Ocean crust age increases symmetrically away from ridge axis.

## Plate Boundaries & Characteristic Features

- **Divergent (Constructive):** Decompression melting of asthenospheric mantle -> basaltic volcanism, pillow lavas, sheeted dykes, normal fault grabens, shallow earthquakes (e.g. Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift).
- **Convergent (Destructive) - Ocean/Continent (Andean):**
- • Subducting slab creates deep ocean trench and **Benioff zone** (earthquakes from shallow to deep down to 700 km).
- • Flux melting (water released by dehydration of slab lowers mantle solidus) produces intermediate andesitic/dacitic arc volcanism.
- • Accretionary wedge of scraped-off sediments.
- **Convergent - Continent/Continent (Himalayan):** Intense crustal shortening, folding, reverse/thrust faults, regional metamorphism, high mountain plateau, no volcanism, shallow/intermediate earthquakes.
- **Conservative / Transform:** Lateral displacement, strike-slip faulting, shallow earthquakes, no volcanism (e.g. San Andreas Fault).

## Plate Driving Forces

- **Slab Pull:** Cold, dense, subducted oceanic lithosphere undergoes eclogite metamorphism (very dense), sinking into asthenosphere under gravity and pulling the rest of the plate behind it. **Dominant driving force (>90%).**
- **Ridge Push:** Gravitational sliding of elevated, buoyant newly formed oceanic crust at mid-ocean ridge downslope away from the ridge axis.
- **Basal Drag:** Minor traction from mantle convection currents dragging base of lithospheric plate.

## Hotspots & Mantle Plumes

- Stationary thermal upwellings originating from core-mantle boundary (D'' layer).
- As tectonic plate moves over the fixed hotspot, a **linear chain of volcanoes** forms with progressive age increase away from the active volcanic centre (e.g. Hawaiian-Emperor seamount chain).
- Direction of plate motion is towards the oldest volcano; rate of plate motion = distance / age difference.

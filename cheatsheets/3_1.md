## Fossil Preservation & Taphonomy

- **Fossil:** Any preserved remains, impression, or trace of organisms from past geological ages.
- **Favourable preservation factors:** Hard skeleton/shell (calcite, silica, apatite), rapid burial (prevents scavenging and mechanical destruction), low energy environment, anoxic bottom waters (inhibits bacterial decay).
- **Preservation modes:**
- • *Replacement / Petrifaction:* Original mineral replaced molecule by molecule (e.g. pyritisation, silicification).
- • *Recrystallisation:* Unstable aragonite converts to coarse calcite crystals.
- • *Moulds & Casts:* Shell dissolves leaving external mould (impressing wall) and internal mould (steinkern); infilled with sediment/mineral = cast.
- • *Carbonisation:* Volatiles expelled leaving thin black carbon film (plants, graptolites).
- • *Trace fossils (ichnofossils):* Tracks, trails, burrows, coprolites, borings; classify behavior (feeding, dwelling, resting, locomotion).

## Life Assemblage vs Death Assemblage

- **Life Assemblage (In Situ / Biocoenosis):**
- • Organisms preserved in living position (e.g. upright corals, bivalves in burrows).
- • Articulated multi-part skeletons (complete trilobites with free cheeks attached; bivalves with valves closed).
- • Wide size distribution / growth stages (juveniles and adults together).
- • Unbroken, unworn delicate spines/frills preserved.
- **Death Assemblage (Transported / Thanatocoenosis):**
- • Disarticulated valves/segments (isolated bivalve valves, separated trilobite cranidia/pygidia).
- • Hydrodynamically sorted (all shells same size/weight).
- • Broken, abraded, rounded shell fragments.
- • Current alignment of elongate fossils (graptolites, belemnites, tentaculites).

## Key Fossil Groups & Paleoecological Morphologies

- **Trilobites (Arthropods, Cambrian–Permian):** Cephalon (head), thorax (segmented), pygidium (tail). Glabella, compound eyes, facial sutures. Pelagic/swimmer (huge eyes, streamlined), Benthic crawler (medium eyes, sturdy legs), Burrower (no eyes, spade-shaped cephalon).
- **Brachiopods (Cambrian–Recent):** Bilateral symmetry perpendicular to hinge line (plane cuts through middle of both valves). Pedicle valve larger with pedicle foramen (anchored to sea floor), brachial valve supports lophophore filter-feeding organ.
- **Bivalves (Molluscs, Cambrian–Recent):** Bilateral symmetry between the two valves (valves are mirror images of each other). Pallial sinus presence = deep burrower with retractable siphons; thick ribbed shell = high energy shallow marine.
- **Corals (Cnidarians):** Calcareous skeleton (corallite), septa (radiating radial plates), tabulae (horizontal floor plates). Solitary (horn corals e.g. Zaphrentis) vs colonial. Shallow, warm (>20°C), clear, agitated, normal marine salinity reef environments.
- **Graptolites (Hemichordates, Cambrian–Devonian):** Planktonic colonial organisms. Stipes composed of thecae housing zooids. Rapid morphological evolution: Ordovician multi-stiped pendent/horizontal -> Silurian single-stiped scandent (Monograptus). Excellent zone fossils for deep marine shales.

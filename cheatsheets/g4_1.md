# Topic G4.1 · Concentration of Natural Resources
**Specification Section:** Interpreting the Geological Record  
**Key Topics:** Magmatic, hydrothermal, placer, and residual ore formation  

---

## Magmatic & Hydrothermal Ore Deposits

- **Magmatic segregation / Immiscibility:** Dense sulfide or oxide liquids separate from mafic magma and sink to base of intrusion (e.g. Bushveld chromite, Sudbury Ni-Cu).
- **Hydrothermal veins:** Superheated volatile-rich aqueous fluids scavenge metals from cooling plutons and country rocks, precipitating ore minerals in fracture veins as pressure and temperature drop.
- • Mineral zoning away from granite intrusion: Sn (cassiterite) & W (wolframite) closest (highest T) -> Cu (chalcopyrite) -> Zn (sphalerite) -> Pb (galena) -> Fe (pyrite) furthest.
- **Porphyry deposits:** Massive low-grade copper/molybdenum deposits associated with subduction arc plutons; pervasive stockwork veinlet network.

## Placer & Residual Deposits

- **Placer deposits:** Mechanical concentration by fluvial/coastal currents.
- • Requires minerals that are: **Dense** (high relative density), **Chemically inert** (resistant to weathering), and **Mechanically hard** (resist abrasion).
- • Examples: Native Gold (RD 19.3), Diamond (H 10), Cassiterite (SnO2, RD 7.0), Magnetite, Zircon.
- • Concentrated at inside of meander bends, downstream of confluences, behind rock bars, and plunge pools.
- **Residual deposits (Bauxite & Kaolinite):**
- • *Bauxite (Al ore):* Extreme tropical weathering leaches silica, leaving insoluble hydrous aluminium oxides (gibbsite).
- • *Kaolinite (China clay):* Hydrothermal or deep weathering breakdown of feldspar in granites.
